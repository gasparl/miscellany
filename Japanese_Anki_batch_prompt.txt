RESEARCH AND GENERATION
1. Grammar is the required target. Research each point's formation, attachment, meaning, nuance, register and distinct uses, consulting JLPT Sensei and cross-checking subtleties. Make at least three genuinely different candidates per point, more when its constructions or senses justify them. Together, they should cover the point thoroughly, not merely paraphrase one use.
2. Vocabulary is an optional pool. Use a supplied entry only where it fits naturally and illustrates its intended sense. Vary your choices across the pool; ideally, each used entry appears in two or three candidates.
3. Verify vocabulary readings, parts of speech, senses, collocations and register with reliable dictionaries (e.g. JMdict/Jisho, Wiktionary or a Japanese dictionary). Check each phrase's grammar and vocabulary sense. Write original, natural, fairly short Japanese in plausible settings. Omit unnecessary common pronouns (especially sentence-initial 彼/彼女); where natural, vary verb forms beyond the dictionary form (e.g. 来[こ]ない, 来[こ]なかった, 来[こ]られる). For words beyond my list, favor intermediate vocabulary (N3 to accessible N2). Do not force pairings, present your sentence as a source's example, or copy long passages or batches of examples.
4. Give each phrase a faithful English translation that follows the Japanese wording and order closely, even if the English sounds a little unnatural. Do not silently supply a subject, object or article absent from the Japanese: put implied English words in square brackets, such as [I], [it] or [the/a]. You can use short parentheses for a nuance, literal gloss or alternative, without letting them overwhelm the main translation. When multiple Japanese expressions could answer the English-to-Japanese card, add a compact sense cue where helpful. For example: 論[ろん]文[ぶん]を 読[よ]み 込[こ]む → "[the/a] thesis/paper to read thoroughly/deeply/repeatedly"; 空振[からぶ]りしても 続[つづ]ける → "even if you miss/fail (~end in failure) [to] continue (~keep going)". Following Anki's formatting, use half-width square-bracket furigana in phrase, for example 片[かた]付[づ]ける; in sentences, put a space before a separately annotated kanji group, for example この 本[ほん]を 読[よ]んだ.
5. The explanation is usually multiple lines, with real newlines and useful indentation. Give each target's title and central meaning its own line. Explain grammar formation/attachment, nuance or restrictions, contrasts, and short glossed examples as justified; for complex points, include relevant forms and negatives. Keep explanations consistent across a grammar point's candidates, adding a sentence-specific note if needed. Separate the vocabulary summary from grammar with a blank line. Combine close synonyms with slashes and distinct senses with semicolons; retain verified supplied senses, marking uncommon/contextual uses. Give an attested noun's する-verb use a separate line; do not invent one. When useful and supported, add one brief kanji-origin or component-meaning line for a kanji expression; and/or very brief etymology for any expression.

EXPLANATION STYLE EXAMPLES
These examples from my earlier notes show the desired detail and line layout. Do not treat them as targets unless listed above.

Example A: a vocabulary word together with a grammar point
phrase:
部屋[へや]はもう 片[かた]付[づ]けてある。
translation:
As for the room, [someone] has already tidied [it] up (~and left it tidy).
explanation:
片[かた]付[づ]ける: to tidy/clean up; to put away; to settle/resolve (a matter); to deal with/finish (a task); to marry off (less common); to eliminate/kill (someone; contextual).

～てある: An intentional action was done, and its result remains (often as preparation).
    Form: transitive verb in て-form + ある.
    Example: ドアを開ける (to open the door) → ドアが開けてある (the door has been left open).
    Here, 片付けてある says that someone tidied the room and it is now tidy.

Example B: a grammar point with different attachments and negative forms
phrase:
彼[かれ]はプロだそうだ。
translation:
He is a professional, I hear (~reportedly).
explanation:
そうだ (hearsay): "I heard that..."; "it is said that..."
    Reports information from another source, not the speaker's visual impression.
    Form: attach そうだ to a plain-form clause. Nouns and な-adjectives retain だ.
    Verb: 来るそうだ → "I hear (he) is coming."
    い-adjective: 忙しいそうだ → "I hear (he) is busy."
    な-adjective: 元気だそうだ → "I hear (he) is well."
    Noun: 学生だそうだ → "I hear (he) is a student."
    Negative: put the ordinary plain negative before そうだ.
        Verb: 来ないそうだ → "I hear (he) is not coming."
        い-adjective: 忙しくないそうだ → "I hear (he) is not busy."
        な-adjective: 元気じゃないそうだ → "I hear (he) is not well."
        Noun: 学生じゃないそうだ → "I hear (he) is not a student."

Another vocabulary-summary pattern, when the noun and する verb both exist:
掃除[そうじ]: cleaning; sweeping up; dusting.
掃除[そうじ]する: to clean; to sweep up.
Kanji: 掃 ("sweep") + 除 ("remove").

A simpler point needs fewer lines; for distinct senses (e.g. そうだ as hearsay versus appearance), keep the attachment rules separate.
